Your project, in plain English

A CPU runs one process at a time. The scheduler chooses who goes next. Here, you enter small imaginary processes, choose a goal, and check whether four scheduling policies meet it. The interesting part: an automatic search tries to find a workload where a policy fails.

FCFS

Arrival order. A long first job can hold everyone up.

SJF

Shortest ready job first. Runs each selected job to completion.

Priority

Lowest priority number first. Also non-preemptive.

Round Robin

Each job gets a short turn, then returns to the queue.

Model: one CPU, one burst per process, no I/O. Process types select which jobs a goal applies to; they do not change scheduling order. Exact burst lengths are known in this simulation.

About “AI”: a supervised decision tree learns scheduler recommendations from 192 simulator-labelled workloads and is evaluated on 48 separate workloads. Its prediction is checked against measured results. Goal wording still uses a constrained rule-based interpreter; there is no LLM or neural network. “No ready process may wait longer than …” checks a finite ready-wait bound; it does not prove starvation freedom.
